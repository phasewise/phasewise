Most AEC project management software was built for architects or contractors, not landscape architects. Before you sign a two-year contract, it's worth understanding exactly where each platform breaks down for your workflow.

## Why Generic AEC Tools Keep Failing Landscape Firms

The core problem is phase structure. A typical AEC platform assumes you're running SD, DD, CD, and CA — four phases, clean handoffs, mostly building-centric deliverables. Landscape projects don't work that way. You're tracking planting plans, irrigation design, grading, hardscape, and plant procurement all simultaneously, often across six or eight phases that bleed into each other.

Platforms like Procore and Autodesk Construction Cloud are genuinely excellent for what they do. But they're built around submittals, RFIs, and punch lists tied to a general contractor's workflow. If you're a five-person landscape firm doing municipal streetscapes and private residential work, you're paying for a system designed for a $50M vertical construction project.

The other failure mode is financial tracking. Most AEC tools track budget against a single contract value. Landscape firms frequently have reimbursables, consultant pass-throughs for civil or MEP coordination, and plant procurement costs that sit outside the design fee. Tools that can't separate those cleanly will give you misleading utilization data.

## The Platforms Worth Evaluating

These are the tools that actually come up when landscape firms are shopping. I'm not covering every option — just the ones I've seen firms seriously consider or implement.

**Deltek Vantagepoint** is the enterprise option. It handles project accounting, resource planning, and billing in one system. Pricing starts around $55/user/month and scales up fast with add-ons. It's built for AEC firms with 20+ staff who need real ERP functionality. For a 6-person firm, the implementation cost alone — typically $15,000–$40,000 — kills the ROI.

**Monograph** was built specifically for architects and has found traction with landscape firms because the phase-based budgeting actually maps to how design fees work. It handles time tracking, project budgets, and invoicing. The trade-off is that it's light on construction administration features — submittals and RFIs require workarounds.

**BQE Core** sits in the middle. It's more configurable than Monograph and handles billing complexity well, including multiple fee types on a single project. The UI is dense and the learning curve is real. Expect 4–6 weeks before your team is using it consistently.

**Procore** makes sense if you're doing significant CA work and your GC is already on it. The collaboration tools are genuinely good. But you're paying $375+/month at minimum, and you'll still need a separate tool for design-phase financial tracking.

## How to Calculate the Real Cost of a Platform Switch

Sticker price is the least important number. Here's what actually matters:

**Total first-year cost formula:**

> Annual subscription + Implementation/onboarding fees + Staff training hours × average hourly rate + Data migration cost

Worked example for a 7-person firm switching to BQE Core:

- Annual subscription: $35 × 7 × 12 = **$2,940**
- Onboarding package: **$1,500**
- Training time: 6 hours × 7 staff × $85/hr blended rate = **$3,570**
- Data migration (historical projects): **$800** (internal time)
- **Total first-year cost: ~$8,810**

That same firm staying on a $29/user tool they already know costs $2,436/year. The switch needs to recover $6,374 in efficiency gains or billing accuracy to break even in year one. That's a real number to put in front of your partners before you decide.

## Construction Administration: Where Most Platforms Fall Short

CA is where landscape firms lose the most money on bad software. The problem is that submittal logs, RFI tracking, and punch lists require coordination between your office, the GC, and sometimes the owner — and most design-phase tools don't handle that communication layer.

Monograph, for example, has no native submittal log. Firms using it for CA end up maintaining a separate spreadsheet or using a shared Google Sheet, which means you're paying for software and still doing manual tracking. That's a workflow failure, not a user error.

Procore handles CA extremely well, but you're paying for the whole platform to use the CA module. If your firm is in CA on three projects at a time and design-phase on eight others, the math often doesn't work.

The practical answer for most mid-size landscape firms is a tool that handles both design-phase financial tracking and basic CA documentation — submittals, RFIs, punch lists — without requiring two separate subscriptions.

## Utilization Rate Tracking: The Feature Firms Undervalue

Every platform in this comparison tracks time. Almost none of them surface utilization rate in a way that's actionable without exporting to Excel.

Utilization rate = Billable hours ÷ Total available hours. If your target is 65% and your senior designer is running at 48%, that's a pipeline problem or a scope-creep problem — and you need to see it in real time, not at month-end.

The firms I've seen manage this well are checking utilization weekly, not monthly. That requires a dashboard that surfaces the number without three clicks and a CSV export. When you're evaluating platforms, ask the vendor to show you exactly how utilization rate is displayed for individual staff members. If they pivot to showing you a time entry screen, that's your answer.

## Common Mistakes Firms Make When Evaluating AEC Software

**Evaluating based on demos, not actual workflows.** Sales demos show the platform at its best. Ask to run your own project data through a trial before you commit. Specifically: import your last three invoices and see if the fee structure maps correctly.

**Underestimating training time for existing staff.** A platform switch on a 6-person team typically costs 30–50 hours of productive time across the team in the first month. That's real project hours lost.

**Choosing the tool the GC or owner's rep uses.** Procore is great for contractors. That doesn't mean it's right for your firm's internal operations. You can collaborate with a GC on Procore without running your entire firm on it.

**Ignoring data portability.** If you leave the platform in three years, can you export your project history, time entries, and invoices in a usable format? Ask this question before you sign. Some platforms make export painful by design.

**Conflating accounting software with project management software.** QuickBooks is not a project management tool. Neither is FreshBooks. If you're running project financials through your accounting software, you're missing phase-level budget visibility that directly affects your profitability.

**Buying for the firm you want, not the firm you have.** A 4-person firm does not need Deltek Vantagepoint. The implementation overhead will consume the productivity gains for two years.
